# Validation Brief

> Module 2 · Validation. Frame the riskiest assumption, then build to test it. **This locks your scenario, no switching after M2.**

## Scenario

Scenario 01 · The Retention Engine

## Riskiest assumption

_The one belief that, if false, sinks the idea._

Growth PMs and customer teams will act earlier on churn if they can clearly see the top churn drivers, supporting evidence, affected accounts, and recommended intervention in one workflow.

## Hypothesis

> We believe **We will change the Growth PM workflow from reviewing disconnected churn metrics after accounts drop off to a guided churn diagnosis flow that identifies the leading churn driver, shows the evidence behind it, surfaces affected accounts, and recommends the next intervention to test.** will cause **More new accounts reach first value in week one because Growth PMs can identify the leading churn driver, target the right at-risk accounts, and test the right intervention earlier.** for **Growth PMs and Customer Success managers**. We'll know we're right when **Users can identify delayed first value as the leading churn driver, prioritize accounts that have not reached the “aha” action, and choose an intervention test aimed at improving week-one activation from 22% toward 40%.**.

## Risk type

- [x] Value (do they want it?)
- [ ] Usability (can they use it?)
- [ ] Feasibility (can we build it?)
- [ ] Viability (should we?)

## Kill switch

_The result that would make you stop or pivot._

If users cannot connect delayed first value to the right stalled accounts or choose an intervention that targets week-one activation, then the prototype fails and the team should pivot.

## The three ingredients

- **Real data (domain metrics):**
  - 30%, 90-day churn: share of new accounts gone within 3 months.
  - 22%, Activation rate: reach the "aha" action in week one.
  - 1.4, Seats active / account: adoption rarely spreads past the buyer.
  - 6.2 days, Time-to-first-value: median, vs. 1-day target.
  - $1.1M, ARR at risk in the next renewal window.
- **User voice (verbatim quotes):**
  - "I signed up, poked around for ten minutes, and never figured out what it actually did for my team.", Ops lead, churned day 12
  - "Nobody on my team adopted it, so I stopped logging in. It felt like one more tool to babysit.", Eng manager, churned day 47
  - "The value was probably in there somewhere, but I needed it to prove itself in week one, not month three.", Founder, churned day 63
- **Hypothesis (above):** ✓
